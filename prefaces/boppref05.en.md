# EXPLANATION OF THE ABBREVIATIONS

<sub>[page VIII]</sub>

r. is 1) root. 2) rule.
Pan. is Pânini.
Ragh. is the Raghu-Vansa (ed. Stenzler).
Ram. is the Râmâyana, Serampore ed.
R. Schl. is Schlegel's edition of the Râmâyana.
Rigv. is the first book of the Rigveda-Sanhitâ (edited in Sanskrit and Latin by Fr. Rosen, whose version I have appended at the cited passages).
Rigv. V. is the fifth book of the Rigvêda, examples from which, drawn from the hymns of Vasishtha, Westergaard adduces in his book entitled *Radices Sanscritae*. (\*)
Rigv. Sp. is Fr. Rosen's Rigva-Specimen.
s. is suffix.
Sa. is the episode of Sâvitrî. (\*\*)
Sak. is the Sakuntalâ, ed. Chézy.
Su. is Sunda and Upasunda. (\*\*\*)
Th. is theme (stem).
Up. is Upakôsa (in the book «Pâtaliputra», ed. Brockhaus).
Ur. is Urvasî (ed. Lenz, Berlin, at Ferd. Dümmler's).
V. is the grammarian Vôpadêva, whose corpus of roots, entitled *Kavikalpadruma*, W. Carey edited in his grammar.
Westerg. is Westergaard's book entitled *Radices sanscritae*.

---

(\*) I have taken account of the Vedic dialect only in those roots whose meanings could not be confirmed from passages of the books of the classical language.

(\*\*) In my book «Diluvium», Berlin, at F. Dümmler's.

(\*\*\*) In my book «Ardschuna's Reise zu Indra's Himmel» (Arjuna's Journey to Indra's Heaven). Berlin, at Wilhelm Logier's.
