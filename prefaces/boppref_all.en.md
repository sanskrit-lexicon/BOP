## Page 02 — Preface, 1 (vol. 1)

<sub>Source (scan): [bop_pref1.jpg](https://sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/csldev/csldoc/build/dictionaries/prefaces/boppref/boppref02.html)</sub>

This book contains all the roots of the Sanskrit language and the most commonly used words, compared with Greek, Latin, Germanic, Lithuanian, Slavic, and Celtic words, and, as far as has been possible, illustrated with passages from Sanskrit books. Many roots, however, have not yet been found among the authors; some occur only in the Vedic dialect; others are confirmed by cognate languages. Thus the obsolete root स्तिघ्, to which the grammarians assign the notion of "ascending," I corroborate by our (German) *steigen*, by the Greek στείχω, ἔστιχον, by the Russian *stignu* "I attain" (= स्तिघ्नोमि), by the Lithuanian *staigio-s* "I hasten," and by the Hibernian (Irish) *staighre* "step, stair." The root क्षुय् "to go" we now see confirmed by the old dialect of the Persian language, where *śiyu* (\*) likewise means "to go," whence *aśiyawam* "I went, I set out," which occurs frequently in the memorable Behistun inscription that the most distinguished gentleman H. C. Rawlinson recently edited and ingeniously interpreted in the work *Journal of the Royal Asiatic Society of Great Britain and Ireland, Vol. X.*

---

(\*) *śiyu* with an *i* inserted, which is always placed before the semivowel *y* whenever a consonant other than *h* precedes it (the pronoun *tya* excepted), as for example *aniya* "other" = अन्य; but we say *thahyâmahya* (with the Parasmaipada ending = Vedic मसि) from the root *thah*, which I would refer not, with Benfey (*Die Persischen Keilinschriften* p. 83, s. v. *that*), to Skt. गाह्, but rather to चत्र् and चकास्, so that *h* corresponds to Sanskrit स्, and *th* to Sanskrit च = *tsch*, as in *thastana*, which Benfey aptly refers to the Sanskrit चेष्ट (l. c. p. 83). As regards the sibilant which in the root *śiyu* corresponds to Sanskrit क्ष्, compare the Zend *pĕrĕśâmi* and the New Persian پرسم *pursem* = Skt. पृच्छामि "I ask," as well as the old Persian root *as* "to come," whence *arasam* "I came," which occurs frequently in the Behistun inscription and corresponds to Sanskrit आर्छम्. I judge that the aspirated sibilant is employed in the root *śiyu* on account of the following vowel *i*, which not infrequently carries aspiration over into the preceding consonant, as for example *amśiya* "I am" as against *amahya*. Thus *d꞉* (so, with Holzmann — *Beiträge zur Erklärung der persischen Keilinschriften* p. 61 — we read in place of Rawlinson's *t꞉* and Lassen's *k'h*) serves only before the vowel *i* in place of the Sanskrit ड् and ध्, as for example in *yad꞉iya* "if," *prid꞉iya* χαῖρε "hail, be well," of which the former agrees with the Sanskrit यदि, while the latter matches the Vedic and Zend forms in its ending, as श्रुधि, ـشـنـيـذـی *śtûidhi* (Comparative Grammar §. 450). To the Sanskrit there correspond *g'* and *g꞉* (so, with Holzmann, we read in place of Rawlinson's *jh, j* and Lassen's *z', g'*), of which the former occurs before *a*, the latter before *i*; thus for example *g'ad꞉iya* "slay, kill" = जहि and ह्धि, *g'iwa* "life" = जीव.

## Page 03 — Preface, 2 (vol. 1)

<sub>Source (scan): [bop_pref2.jpg](https://sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/csldev/csldoc/build/dictionaries/prefaces/boppref/boppref03.html)</sub>

<sub>[page VI]</sub>

Many of the forms admitted among the roots by the Indian grammarians are not true roots, but stems of denominative verbs, as for example कुमार्, कर्त्, मन्त्र्, स्तोम्; some contain a reduplicative syllable, as for example ज्रन्द्, which arose from घस् with the *a* expelled, जाग्र् or rather जाग्र् (Gr. ἐγείρω). For all roots that end in the vowel ऋ in fact end [in *ar*], which in the stronger forms is retained — thus for example कृत, कृत्वा, कर्तुम् from कृ, i. e. कर्. Likewise roots terminating in the letter ऋ in truth end in अर्, which in the weaker forms they contract into ऋ; these, according to the opinion of the native grammarians, produce the letter ऋ, but in the weaker forms they change it into इर्, or, by reason of a preceding labial, into उर्, ऊर्; thus for example किरामि, चकर्, चकार्, कीर्य्ये, कीर्ण, from कृ, i. e. कर्; पिपर्मि, पुरु, पूर्य्ये, पूर्ण, from पृ, i. e. पर्. The verbs of the ninth class, those roots to which the grammarians assign the vowel ऋ, in the special tenses shorten the syllable अर् into ऋ, e. g. जाणामि from ज्ॄ, i. e. जर्. The view which I set out more fully on this matter in the book entitled *Vocalismus* (pp. 177–193), and which I sought to corroborate from the Zend and the cognate European languages, I now see confirmed by the language of the ancient Persians, which retains the syllable *ar* intact in its forms, whereas Sanskrit shortens it into ऋ, or changes it into इर्, ईर्, उर्, ऊर्; so that, for example, *barta* "borne," *karta* "made," *adarśnuś* (\*) "he dared," *asariyata* "he was slain" correspond to the Sanskrit forms भृत, कृत, अधृष्णोत्, अशीर्यत (var. lect. शॄ, i. e. शर्).

The books which have chiefly served for the illustration of this glossary are these: 1) *Indralókamanam* (\*\*). 2) The Deluge, together with three other most excellent episodes of the Mahâ-Bhârata, Berlin 1829, at Ferdinand Dümmler's. 3) Nala, an episode of the Mahâ-Bhârata (2nd ed., Berlin 1832, in Fr. Nicolai's bookshop). 4) *Bhagavadgîta*. 5) *Hitópadêsa*. 6) Urvasî, a play of Kâlidâsa.

---

(\*) Final *t* after a preceding vowel *u* passes into *ś꞉*, but after other vowels it is dropped; for which reason, in place of *parʾuviyat* (Rawlinson, N. R. 8. 45, *hacâ parʾuviyat* "from of old"), I hold that *parʾuviyata* should be read, since by its suffix it agrees with the Sanskrit forms in तस् (as for example अतस्, इतस्, शत्रुतस्). The true ablatives, however, though they agree in form with the instrumental, are *kabuǵiyaś* "Cambyses" (Rawl. I. 40) and *darʾugâ* (IV. 37). Likewise *bâbirʾuś* "Babylon" (II. 65) is a true ablative, and rests, like *tanaôt* (Comparative Grammar §. 180). The form of the genitive *bâbirʾuwa* agrees with the Sanskrit genitives such as धेन्वास्, with *â* shortened and *ś* dropped, as always after a preceding vowel *a* or *â*.

(\*\*) Arjuna's Journey to Indra's Heaven, together with other Episodes of the Mahâ-Bhârata, Berlin 1824, at W. Logier's.
